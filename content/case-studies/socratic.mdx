<CaseText id="problem" className="pt-64" sectionHeading="The Problem" title="Chat AI fails writers because writing is spatial. Chat is linear.">
Generic AI writing tools produce <Def definition="generic-outputs">generic outputs</Def> because they skip the thinking that makes writing personal. For scholarship essays especially, the quality of the thought behind the words determines the outcome - not the polish.

Chat interfaces compound this with a <Def definition="black-box">black box experience</Def>: users can't see the reasoning, can't compare alternatives side by side, and can't organize their thinking spatially the way writing actually works.
</CaseText>

<CaseText className="pb-64" sectionHeading="The Challenge">
How do you make AI collaboration feel **transparent and generative**, not prescriptive? And how do you ship it in 7 days?
</CaseText>

<CaseText className="py-48" sectionHeading="Discovery" title="We asked 10 people on campus about their experience with chat-AI interfaces.">
We found that the root cause isn't the AI, but a fundamental <Def definition="mental-model">mental model mismatch</Def> between how AI chat works and how writing actually happens in practice.

This produces <Def definition="failure-modes">three failure modes</Def> in chat-based AI writing tools - and all three pointed to the same solution: give users more agency over the process.
</CaseText>
</CaseGroup>

<CaseGroup id="initial-designs" className="flex flex-col gutter-lg">
<CaseText sectionHeading="Initial Designs" title="Questions, not answers.">
The core of Socratic.ai is a <Def definition="questioning-model">Socratic questioning model</Def>: instead of writing for users, the AI asks targeted questions that help them articulate what's already there. The output is authentically theirs because the thinking was theirs.
</CaseText>
<CaseMedia size="small" aspectRatio="video" caption="Socratic questioning in action" className="pt-0 pb-4" asset="appDemo" />

<CaseText title="From thread to canvas.">
We replaced the chat thread with a <Def definition="vector-canvas">vector canvas</Def> that lets users place, compare, group, and navigate their ideas the way writing actually works - non-linearly, spatially, iteratively.
</CaseText>
<CaseMedia size="small" caption="Canvas interface - spatial organization of AI insights and drafts" className="pt-0 pb-4" asset="canvas" />

<CaseText title="Surfacing what the prompt doesn't say.">
Scholarship prompts are designed to be open-ended, but <Def definition="winning-essays">winning scholarship essays</Def> carry hidden criteria that committees never write down. Socratic.ai surfaces those criteria during the questioning process, so users write toward them without being told what to say.
</CaseText>

<CaseText id="closing" sectionHeading="Closing" title="Final thoughts and learnings">
<CaseThought title="Constraints made the decisions easier">
I expected seven days to feel limiting. Instead, it removed a lot of the noise. Every decision had to be **justified right away**, so there was no time to second-guess or over-explore. The design ended up sharper than it probably would have been with more runway.
</CaseThought>

<CaseThought title="Show reasoning to make AI trustworthy">
Users weren't skeptical of the outputs as much as they were skeptical of the process. Once they could see why the AI was asking a question, they **trusted it more** instead of just answering to get past it.
</CaseThought>

<CaseThought title="Being a good PM meant staying out of the way">
I learned that to lead is to empower instead of just coordinating people. Writing clear requirements, making scope calls early, and keeping async communication tight let people work without waiting on me.
</CaseThought>
</CaseText>
