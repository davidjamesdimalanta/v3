<CaseText id="problem" className="pt-32" sectionHeading="Overview" title="What is Social Prescribing?">
**Social prescribing** is a structured way to route people from a clinical encounter to non-clinical community supports such as 
housing, fitness, food security, and social connection. 

A **Link Worker** is the human liason connecting people to the community supports, often working in a SALC.

A **SALC** is a community centre that provides social prescribing services.

**OACAO** is the provincial body that funds the centres and collects their year-end data.

</CaseText>

<CaseText sectionHeading="The Problem" title="Every SALC did their year-end report by hand.">
In interviews, Link Workers described having to cross-reference paper binders and Excel sheets for patient data to report, **taking days to do the report**.
</CaseText>

<CaseText sectionHeading="The Challenge" className="pb-32">
How do we reduce the time spent on the year-end reporting for link workers, **while giving OACAO's high quality data for funding grants?**
</CaseText>

<CaseText sectionHeading="Discovery" title="Every centre tracks differently.">
Interviewing 10 SALCs, each described a different way of managing clients, but OACAO's year-end report asked for data they didn't keep in one place, or didn't keep at all.

> "I don't know if you know all about the reporting process, but it's, it's like a three-day event to try and do the final report."

</CaseText>

<CaseText sectionHeading="A Constraint" title="The service dealt with PHI">
Personal health information in Ontario is governed by two laws: **PHIPA**, which regulates how health information custodians collect, use, and disclose patient data, and **PIPEDA**, Canada's federal private-sector privacy law. **Our designs had to comply with both, or OACAO could not proceed with our work**.
</CaseText>
</CaseGroup>

<CaseGroup id="decision" variant="discovery">
<CaseFixed sectionHeading="The Key Decision" title="Choosing which part of the service to improve">
Looking at the service end-to-end, we identified the following bottlenecks:

**Link workers**
 - SALCs didn't have a *standardardized* client sheet
 - they were doing reporting twice (once on paper forms, and once on surveymonkey)

**OACAO**
 - SALCs would sometimes skip reporting and simply email them with client lists and receipts
 - They had to manually aggregate due to multiple sources of data

Overall, we decided LinkLog helped Link Workers most when it **was easy to get started with and simplified year-end reporting**, and for OACAO, it was most rewarding to be on the **same platform as Link Workers**, and to get all **reports from every SALC in one place**.

Some design opportunities we did not pursue due to lack of impact & time included improving client onboarding and client follow-up workflows.

<CaseGroup id="implementation" variant="discovery">
<CaseFixed sectionHeading="The Build" title="Building the platform over the weekend">

We used a lot of AI, but in targeted and meaningful ways. It was important to **validate its output across models**, but what was more crucial to our success was **communicating our intent through carefully crafted prompts**.

We shipped a working platform instead of a Figma prototype to showcase how our solution made real impact, doing so through scenario walkthroughs as the link worker and OACAO.

<CaseGroup id="policy-stack" variant="discovery">
<CaseFixed sectionHeading="The Build" title="How policy affected the tech stack">
The same **PHIPA** and **PIPEDA** rules that shaped the service also chose our tech stack. We picked tools built for handling health data, so every privacy promise was enforced by LinkLog itself.

Each centre's records stay walled off from every other centre's. Personal details are stripped out before any data is shared or reported, and they never appear in the system's logs.

And because small groups can be enough to identify someone, OACAO's reports automatically hide any count under five.
