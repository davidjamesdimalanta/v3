<CaseText id="problem" className="pt-64" sectionHeading="The Problem" title="10 out of 10 SALCs compile their year-end report by hand.">
Every Seniors Active Living Centre we interviewed told us the same thing: reporting to OACAO is a paper chase that runs once a year and eats days at a time. Meanwhile, the actual work of <Def definition="social-prescribing">Social Prescribing</Def> - calling clients, checking in, building trust - gets squeezed by the work of proving that work happened.
</CaseText>

<CaseText className="pb-64" sectionHeading="The Challenge">
How do you give Link Workers more time to do the relational work that defines their role - **while making it easier, not harder, for OACAO to prove the programme deserves another year of funding?**
</CaseText>

<CaseText sectionHeading="Discovery" title="Ad-Hoc processes">
Speaking to 10 SALCs in the winter, we learned that they each had their own workflow for client management, but OACAO's year-end report demanded info they didn't always have at hand.

> "I don't know if you know all about the reporting process, but it's, it's like a three-day event to try and do the final report."

Our goal then was to reduce the cost of reporting without making the everyday client relationship more bureaucratic.
</CaseText>

<CaseText
sectionHeading="Constraint"
title="The Backstage: Policy">
With <Def definition="phipa-pipeda">PHIPA and PIPEDA</Def>, we were dealing with sensitive data, so the service had to make useful reporting possible without turning OACAO into the owner of identifiable client records.
</CaseText>
</CaseGroup>

<CaseText id="closing" sectionHeading="Closing" title="Final thoughts and learnings">
**Service Blueprints Show You Where to Cut**
The current-state blueprint surfaced that the most expensive part of the Link Worker's year wasn't doing the work - it was reconstructing it for OACAO at year-end. Once we saw that, every design decision served closing that gap.

**80% Is Good Enough - If The 80% Closes The Loop**
We chose end-to-end coverage at lower fidelity over one polished flow, because the question we were testing was whether the whole loop could close, not whether one screen looked perfect.

**AI Is A Force Multiplier On Decisions, Not A Substitute For Them**
Codex, Claude Code, and Gemini cut the build time from weeks to days - but they only mattered because the service blueprint, the interviews, and the co-design sessions had already told us what to build. AI didn't shorten the path to the decision; it shortened the path from the decision to a live product.

**What's Next**
Pilot in 2-3 SALCs before the next OACAO funding cycle, finalize the PHIPA-compliant data governance brief, and explore a Link Trainer track to scale onboarding without scaling our team.
</CaseText>
