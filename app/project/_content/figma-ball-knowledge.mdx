<CaseText id="overview" className="pt-64" sectionHeading="Overview" title="Agentic research that knows when to doubt itself.">
Product teams have adopted AI for desk research faster than they've developed guardrails for it. The result is reports that sound authoritative and cite nothing verifiable - competitive matrices built on smoothed summaries that omit the contradictions, the hesitations, the data that would actually change a decision.

Figma Ball Knowledge is a Claude Code skill module that brings structured research methodology into an agentic workflow. It doesn't just generate output. It enforces the quality gates that separate informed strategy from ad-hoc synthesis.
</CaseText>

<CaseText id="problem" className="pt-48" sectionHeading="The Problem" title="AI research tools are filling a market gap with the wrong thing.">
The demand for faster research is real. Product timelines have compressed - two-week sprints don't accommodate longitudinal studies. Teams need insight pipelines that keep pace with development cycles without sacrificing the rigor that separates a strategy from a guess.

What's shipped to meet that demand is mostly prompt wrappers. They produce fluent, well-structured text. They also omit at twice the rate they invent - and because the output reads confidently, stakeholders rarely notice. The tool that should reduce research risk is quietly compounding it.

The market gap isn't speed. Every AI research tool offers speed. The gap is verifiability: outputs with traceable sourcing, mandatory counter-review, and a structure that doesn't hide the weakness in its own conclusions.
</CaseText>

<CaseText className="pb-48" sectionHeading="The Challenge">
How do you build a research agent that's fast enough to fit a sprint cadence, but disciplined enough that a product manager can stake a roadmap decision on its output?
</CaseText>
